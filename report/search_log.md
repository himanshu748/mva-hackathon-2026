# Targeted evidence review record

7–8 September 2026. This was an iterative, AI-assisted targeted review. It is not a systematic search, registered review, novelty certification or proof of absence. Searches were followed by primary-source inspection; inaccessible full text is identified in the ledger. Sources were included when they could change the candidate decision, evidence interpretation, model design, exposure feasibility or safety criteria.

Searches for rapamycin/BubR1 also returned chemical dimerization experiments and cancer-cell pathway studies. These were not treated as evidence that oral rapamycin corrects MVA. BUB1 and BUB1B are different genes; results from BUB1 cancer-expression screens were not conflated with BUB1B loss-of-function rescue. No numerical ranking was assigned by counting papers or heterogeneous effect sizes.

Evidence types remain separate: peer-reviewed primary research, regulatory labeling, original patent disclosure, congress abstract, registry metadata and our own public-data calculations. No efficacy inference was taken from social posts or reviews; those served only as discovery leads when encountered. The TRUTH abstract was checked in the official EASL PDF, including visual inspection of page S9. Its primary endpoint was retained; secondary percentages with unclear denominators were omitted.

The highest-impact new claims were checked during synthesis: patent comparator and limits, allele mapping, TRUTH numerator/denominator and publication status, and neural function versus cell survival. The public-data analysis plan was written before downloading the full count matrix, after reading the paper and its metadata. It was neither blinded nor prospectively preregistered.

## Additional patient-benefit review

After the participant asked whether the proposal could save the child, a second focused review examined direct BUB1B restoration, exact-allele evidence, human outcomes/trials and competing neural-preservation mechanisms. The source ledger and [patient-benefit review](patient_benefit_review.md) record material findings.

Exact coding/genomic ClinVar queries distinguished the submitted c.3006T>G from the same-protein-change c.3006T>A entry. Primary studies separated transformed-cell complementation and genetic SIRT2 survival effects from NMN abundance measurements. The 2026 Drosophila neural study added mitochondrial/proteostasis measurements and a conditional preservation comparison; its rapamycin result concerns neuroblast counts. Approved elamipretide and omaveloxolone were examined through FDA records and primary trial evidence without inferring MVA efficacy. ClinicalTrials.gov disease/gene queries and human case/consensus evidence did not identify demonstrated disease correction or a treatment-attributable survival benefit. Search absence was not treated as proof that no relevant research exists.

These findings changed the baseline measurements and comparison logic. They did not establish a superior MVA medicine or an individualized clinical recommendation. The dated lack of direct rescue evidence is a research gap; the next meaningful step requires appropriate biological models and clinical interpretation.

## Reason for stopping this search wave

Core evidence questions have primary support or a stated access/biological limitation. Additional heterogeneous ageing studies would not establish target burden or benefit in the child's tissues. The remaining useful work is reproducibility checking, artifact consistency and a final source/rules refresh before submission. The current search does not establish that dasatinib is the optimal MVA medicine.

## Candidate challenge, 8 September 2026

Question: does a market-approved compound have a closer human aneuploid cell-preservation precedent than the existing clearance nomination? Parent and separate agents checked drug identity, model, intervention timing, functional endpoints and current labeling.

- Added [Hirata 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-70362-x) and [Nawa 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0219592): sodium phenylbutyrate preservation evidence is preventive, with no established mature-neuron recovery or BUB1B clinical benefit. Main texts were downloaded with hashes; an independent reviewer checked timing and model limits.
- Checked the current [July 2026 PHEBURANE label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=d15f2cbf-4d22-40b5-8ef9-16b9d323a7f9), including indication, parent/metabolite pharmacology and neurotoxicity. No patient-dose calculation was performed.
- Reconciled nominal dasatinib concentration overlap between [Zhu 2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.12344) and [Lombardo 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843680/). Different experimental conditions prevent a safety-margin inference. Added matched concentration-time, unbound-exposure and functional-harm requirements.
- Excluded an attribution error: [Joy 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2021.06.009) reports genetic protection in fly epithelial chromosome-instability models; its mention of rapamycin names TOR, not a tested drug arm.
- Excluded [Hwang 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2019.10.059) from the approved-drug shortlist: experimental lipid-pathway compounds improved human trisomic fibroblast proliferation/morphology but did not remove 53BP1 stress foci; no treated neural-function rescue was established. This is not the NRF2/oltipraz paper.
- Checked [Zamponi 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6156351/) and its supplement: functional rescue belongs to genetically expressed mitochondrial catalase. Oltipraz/sulforaphane were used for NRF2-related molecular validation; those results cannot inherit catalase's functional benefit.

Decision: retain the conditional dasatinib clearance nomination, remove automatic experimental priority, and name sodium phenylbutyrate as a separate comparison for demonstrated protein stress. The comparison is proposed laboratory work. No new biological results or clinical efficacy evidence were generated.
